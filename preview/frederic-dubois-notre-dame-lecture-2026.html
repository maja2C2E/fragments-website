Post-Fire Structural Assessment of Notre-Dame de Paris

Guest lecture by Dr Frédéric Dubois — CNRS / LMGC, Montpellier, France

Date & time

Wednesday, 2 December 2026
10:00–12:00

Venue

Great Council Hall
Faculty of Civil Engineering, University of Zagreb

Language

English

Programme

Lecture followed by an informal gathering and discussion

The Faculty of Civil Engineering, University of Zagreb, and the Croatian Centre for Earthquake Engineering will host a guest lecture by Frédéric Dubois on the structural assessment of Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris following the 2019 fire.

About the lecture

Post-fire structural assessment.

The fire on April 15, 2019, caused major damage to Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris: the destruction of the timber roof framework; the collapse of vault sections into the nave following the fall of the spire; cracks in the vaults and buttress walls; and residual displacements resulting from thermal expansion.

To support the restoration, the French State established a public institution dedicated to the conservation and restoration of Notre-Dame (EP RNDP), while the CNRS and the Ministry of Culture coordinated a scientific project involving experts in stone, wood, metal, glass, acoustics, digital technologies, and structural engineering. Within the “structure” working group, the objectives are to understand the mechanical behavior of the Gothic cathedral, assess its strength and resilience, and develop advanced 3D numerical models to analyze the complex interactions between the vaults, flying buttresses, nave walls, buttresses, and supports.

The lecture will address three main aspects:

  • The original structural behavior of the cathedral.
  • The impact of the fire.
  • The effect of the new roof framework on the stability of the masonry under wind loads.

About the speaker

Frédéric Dubois

Frédéric Dubois holds a PhD in mechanics from the Université de la Méditerranée. He is a research engineer at the French National Centre for Scientific Research (CNRS) and Deputy Director of the Laboratory of Mechanics and Civil Engineering (LMGC) in Montpellier.

He is the architect and principal developer of LMGC90, an open-source software package dedicated to modelling interacting multi-body systems and coupling multiple physical processes. In recognition of his work on the software, he received the CNRS Cristal award in 2007.

His expertise spans computational mechanics, contact mechanics, fracture and non-smooth dynamics, with applications in masonry structures, architectural heritage and material behaviour. He supervises doctoral research and participates in numerous research projects, including work related to the restoration of Notre-Dame Cathedral through the MAESTRO research group associated with CNRS and the French Ministry of Culture.

Organisation

Research and heritage.

The lecture is organised within the framework of the Croatian Centre for Earthquake Engineering’s research project in earthquake engineering and the FragMENTS project, on the occasion of Frédéric Dubois’s visit and the LMGC90 workshop, taking place at the Faculty of Civil Engineering, University of Zagreb, from 2 to 4 December 2026.
