Frédéric Dubois on the restoration of Notre-Dame Cathedral

A guest lecture on the research and engineering challenges of restoring Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris.

When

Wednesday, 2 December 2026
10:00

Where

Great Council Hall
University of Zagreb | Faculty of Civil Engineering

The University of Zagreb | Faculty of Civil Engineering and the Croatian Centre for Earthquake Engineering invite colleagues to a guest lecture by Dr Frédéric Dubois.

The lecture will focus on the restoration of Notre-Dame Cathedral in Paris, offering an opportunity to explore the research and engineering challenges associated with restoring this historic building and the role of scientific research in assessing and preserving architectural heritage.

The lecture will be followed by a discussion and an informal gathering with refreshments.

About the speaker

Frédéric Dubois

Frédéric Dubois holds a PhD in mechanics from the Université de la Méditerranée. He is a research engineer at the French National Centre for Scientific Research (CNRS) and Deputy Director of the Laboratory of Mechanics and Civil Engineering (LMGC) in Montpellier.

He is the architect and principal developer of LMGC90, an open-source software package for modelling interacting multi-body systems with multiple physics coupling. He received the CNRS Cristal award in 2007 in recognition of his work on the software.

His expertise spans computational mechanics, contact mechanics, fracture and non-smooth dynamics, with applications in masonry structures, architectural heritage and material behaviour. He supervises PhD research and participates in numerous research projects, including work related to the restoration of Notre-Dame Cathedral through the MAESTRO research group associated with CNRS and the French Ministry of Culture.

Organisation

Research and heritage.

The lecture is organised within the projects of the Croatian Centre for Earthquake Engineering and the FragMENTS project, as part of Frédéric Dubois’s visit and the LMGC90 workshop at the Faculty from 2 to 4 December 2026.
